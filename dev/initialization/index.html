Preparing a solver start and preventive dispatch

A numerical starting point helps a solver converge. A preventive dispatch sets what equipment will actually do. DroopOPF keeps those two operations explicit. Neither a seed nor a converged solver status alone establishes security.

Initialization and acceptance checks

  1. Complete the source-data checks. Freeze the study contract before choosing a seed. Record the controls and equipment that may change, the objective, allowed preventive dispatch, movement limits, shared parameters, and the training and held-out cases.
  2. Prefer a replay-qualified same-contract state. When testing a new demand, first solve the frozen schedule if one exists. Use its qualified equilibrium and nearby qualified states as deterministic seed candidates. Map voltages and outputs by equipment ID, shift angles to the target reference and check availability, limits and control settings in the target case.
  3. Use saved-profile or flat starts as fallbacks. If no suitable seed is available, try fixed-control feasibility or a loss-only AC OPF as a preparatory solve. Use the resulting primal values to initialize the target model. Keep any freer dispatch, temporary guards, or relaxations in a separate preparation record. Record seed provenance, solver status, residuals, iterations and objective for each attempt.
  4. Compare a local candidate with a known feasible baseline under the same constraints and objective. If the candidate is worse or unresolved, retry from the qualified baseline or a nearby state before selecting the best replay-qualified result. If the baseline violates extra search guards, report that distinction before comparing objectives.
  5. Qualify under the declared physical and control limits. Independently replay AC balance, voltage and angle bounds, both branch terminals, effective capability, active response, equipment and frozen settings. Treat numerical failure as unresolved. Any infeasibility certificate must match the case, demand, and limits it claims to exclude.

If the study needs operating headroom, declare its metric, target and state scope separately from the original limits. Measure its economic cost and verify the achieved headroom in replay. Where a study evaluates a dispatch schedule between its knots, include interior points near binding equipment. Keep frozen-schedule results separate from demand-specific redispatch, even when all controller parameters are shared.

Numerical starting points

Without an initial_state, the AC builder starts voltage magnitudes at 1 pu, angles at zero radians, and generator active/reactive power at their stored initial values. With an ACState, it uses the supplied voltage, angle, active and reactive vectors in case order. These are primal starts; no dual multipliers are supplied. Common-frequency solves otherwise start frequency at the case nominal value.

prepare_initialization copies a supplied state, shifts all angles to the numerical reference, and evaluates the declared controller laws. For solved frequency it uses bounded scalar bisection at the guessed voltages and estimated losses:

\[\sum_i P_i(f;\widehat V)=P_D+\widehat P_{\rm loss}.\]

Here i indexes generators, f is frequency in Hz, P_i is the saturated active response in system-base pu, V̂ is guessed voltage in pu, P_D is total active load, and P̂_loss is estimated active network loss on the same power base. A missing bracket is returned explicitly. It does not prove AC infeasibility. The projected state need not satisfy AC balance; always solve and validate it. For numerical recovery, solve_common_frequency(...; objective_policy=:feasibility) uses a zero objective with the same constraints; its default :dispatch policy retains the dispatch-distance objective.

seed = prepare_initialization(case, plants, responses;
    state=previous_state, frequency_bounds=(58.0, 62.0),
    loss_estimate=estimated_loss_pu, provenance=:previous_ac)
result = solve_common_frequency(case, plants, responses;
    frequency_bounds=(58.0, 62.0),
    initial_state=seed.state, initial_frequency_hz=seed.frequency_hz)
report = validate_common_frequency(case, result)

The numerical example bounds must be replaced with the study's declared bounds. A conventional outage OPF can supply a fallback seed. Its unrestricted dispatch is not an accepted controlled-outage result; the final solve must enforce the original frozen response contract.

Preventive dispatch and reserves

design_preventive_dispatch(...; objective=:reserve) explicitly changes preventive active references. It retains reactive curves, frequency gains and equipment. The nominal base AC state is solved jointly with a common symmetric reserve fraction η of each available responder's effective active range ΔP_i:

\[r_i=\eta\Delta P_i,\qquad \underline P_i\le P_i^0-r_i\le P_i^0+r_i\le\overline P_i.\]

r_i is upward and downward reserve, P_i^0 is preventive output, and the lower and upper bounds are effective active-power limits. All powers use system-base pu. The objective maximizes η, bounded between zero and one half. Both reserve endpoints must respect apparent-power and inverter current capability at the base reactive output and voltage. Reserve also cannot exceed frequency gain (in system pu/Hz) times the smaller allowed deviation from nominal frequency. Zero-gain resources receive no primary-response reserve credit.

A supplied reserve_fraction_target holds that fraction and minimizes normalized preventive redispatch instead. This is a feasible reserve target, not a claim that the maximum reserve has been found.

These are local base-point capability margins. They do not certify delivery through a congested network, energy duration, a frequency nadir or stability. The caller must subsequently freeze the chosen references and assess outages.

Security-aware preventive references

With objective=:security and explicit branch contingencies, the same API couples the base and supplied outage states through common preventive active references. It minimizes squared active redispatch normalized by each generator's source active range (with a 0.001 pu numerical denominator floor). Gains and reactive curves stay fixed. Solved-frequency scenarios obey saturated primary response; prescribed-frequency scenarios retain free conventional OPF dispatch, so these are different operating policies. Neither allows equipment movement here. The security objective defaults to a zero common reserve fraction. Set reserve_fraction_target explicitly when a positive local reserve floor must also be enforced.

validate_preventive_dispatch checks native convergence, each AC/controller state, angle bounds and reserve endpoints. A bounded set of design contingencies is not full security qualification: freeze each candidate and check the complete required catalogue, retaining one common policy across its accepted states.

Shared static witnesses may now select explicit tap_controls and shunt_controls. Their continuous settings are tied across every demand and outage; selected outaged branches have no electrical tap variable in that state. Unselected equipment stays fixed. Validate with the identical control lists. This is preventive sharing, not corrective movement or discrete-bank qualification. Supply legal bank metadata and qualified same-contract starts; retain fixed-equipment baselines for comparison.

Choosing an initialization and design objective

Use the workflow flowcharts to declare the physical contract, numerical start, objective priorities and candidate review criteria. The ordinary OPF/joint builder minimizes weighted squared dispatch deviation. Reserve or loss objectives require an explicitly selected supporting API. A preparatory state needs target-contract replay before it becomes an accepted operating point. Record controls, equipment movement, state scope and seed origin.

Strict staged joint design

Local qualification, 27 November 2026. Use the opt-in API for a single snapshot:

r = optimize_staged_joint_design(case;
    reserve_generator_ids=[1, 2],
    droop_controls=selected_droops,
    tap_controls=selected_taps,
    shunt_controls=selected_banks,
    initial_state=seed,
    hessian="exact",
    initialization_max_iterations=3000,
    initialization_max_seconds=1200.,
    max_iterations=3000,
    max_seconds=1200.)

Only explicitly supplied controls and equipment bounds are available. Existing optimizejointdesign retains its original dispatch-distance objective and behavior. The staged API uses cold zero-objective feasibility, maximum common symmetric Q-box reserve, zero-objective feasibility with that reserve retained, then active-loss minimization. The last objective is total system-base Pg; with fixed load its minimizer is the loss minimizer. Each stage separately reports loss_mw, including fixed shunt consumption in the net balance.

The reserve metric is the minimum of (Qg-Qmin)/(Qmax-Qmin) and (Qmax-Qg)/(Qmax-Qmin) across explicitly selected available generators. Select only nonzero Q ranges. This is source generator Q-box reserve: tighter droop or apparent/current capability may limit it further and are still enforced, but do not redefine this metric. AVR references retain their supplied native reactive-assignment policy. This API does not reproduce the work-machine CLI's CSV classification, AVR scheduling, RMPCT plant sharing, capability preparation or PowerModels model import. Those are separate consumer policies, not implicit changes to core controls or data.

The same model survives all stages. Every primal variable is copied by identity to its next start, including selected droop, equipment and auxiliary values. After reserve initialization, the reserve auxiliary is fixed to zero and its requirement is represented by the retained fixed floor. No dual warm start is claimed. Initialization and design use the same Hessian, tol 1e-8, constraint tolerance 1e-10, zero numerical bound relaxation, bound/slack push and fraction values 1e-9, and disabled acceptable-level early stopping. Model residuals must be <=1e-10; native converged/feasible status and unchanged independent physical replay are also required. Reports retain requested and actual first-iterate residuals, final residuals and all starts/returned primals.

The reserve floor is computed once as max(0, initializedreserve - 1e-8). A zero floor adds no duplicate Q-limit rows. Physical limits and existing replay defaults are not widened. Each stage has its own budget. Failure stops the sequence and returns accepted=false; the last attempted result is not an accepted fallback. Check the wrapper's acceptance, not just the last solver status or validatejoint_design in isolation.

Synthetic coverage exercises variable taps, banks and droop parameters, full primal transfer, budget failure, corrupted returned physics, invalid selections and finite log1pexp derivatives near saturation. Larger public single-snapshot runs use fresh outputs and source-bound inputs. Historical states are numerical hints that require new target-contract replay. Report the accepted reserve floor and the evaluated demand/outage scope explicitly.

Use validatestagedjointdesign(case, r) to independently recheck the complete sequence, original-limit replay, common reserve floor and handoff ledger. The per-stage result.opf.objective records that stage's objective (zero, reserve, or total Pg). The older jointdesignmetrics objective comparison assumes the ordinary dispatch-distance objective; use stage.lossmw for staged loss reporting. The reusable API is local and opt-in; no package release or consumer dependency pin update has been made.

DroopOPF.optimize_staged_joint_design — Function

Opt-in single-snapshot cold feasibility, reactive-reserve initialization, design feasibility and active-loss design. Transfers every primal variable by identity. Each stage requires strict model residual <=1e-10 and independent replay. Returns (accepted, stages, result, reservefloor); failure stops the sequence. Reserve uses explicitly selected source Q boxes; circle constraints remain active but do not define the reserve metric. AVR targets retain supplied assignments. Existing optimizejoint_design behavior and smoothing remain unchanged.
