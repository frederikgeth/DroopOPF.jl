Mixed AVR and Volt/VAR controls

A single network may combine free reactive output, fixed schedules, automatic voltage regulation (AVR) and Volt/VAR droop. The reactive-mode primer explains the distinctions. Every available generator still obeys AC balance and its supplied capability.

Figure 1. AVR adjusts reactive output to a voltage target until capability binds; a droop follows its supplied curve. These are steady-state relations.

Apparent rating and effective reactive limits

Generator(...; s_rated=S) declares apparent rating $S$ in pu on the case system base. Let $P,Q$ be active/reactive output in pu and $Q^{min},Q^{max}$ supplied box limits. An explicit rating adds $P^2+Q^2\le S^2$ without replacing those bounds. For $|P|\le S$, the effective reactive interval intersects the box with $[-\sqrt{S^2-P^2},\sqrt{S^2-P^2}]$. $|\cdot|$ is absolute value and the square root is nonnegative. reactive_limits(generator, p) evaluates this intersection at active output p.

Omitting s_rated preserves the rectangular capability model. PSS/E MBASE is not automatically a verified nameplate rating. An apparent-power circle is not a complete voltage-dependent converter current envelope.

Voltage regulation and sharing

An AVR holds its measured-voltage target while its effective Q interval permits. At a reactive limit it releases voltage regulation. A clipped droop retains its parameters and delivers the effective Q bound. Multiple AVR units at one regulated location require a consistent target and share the same normalized position within their current reactive intervals.

ReactiveControlAssignment makes the mode and regulated location explicit. Pass the same assignments to the solve and independent validation. Legacy attachments and explicit assignments follow their documented compatibility rules; unsupported combinations are rejected rather than silently ignored.

mixed_reactive_controls(case; avr_fraction, ratings, voltage_targets) can select online positive-Pmax units deterministically by descending Pmax and ascending ID, rounding the requested count to nearest integer with half upward. Offline records stay offline. Supply ratings and targets explicitly; the helper does not infer manufacturer capability or controller dynamics.

Shared design and chronological security

The smooth optimize_joint_design, optimize_droop_parameters and optimize_multi_period paths support compatible explicit mixed assignments and selected free legacy curve parameters. A selected curve must match its assignment. Consult solver compatibility for exact parameter-design restrictions.

optimize_multi_period_security couples chronological base dispatch with ramp rates, shared curves and declared outage recourse. Each period's preventive AVR target remains shared across its outages. Optional target bounds and hourly movement limits must be supplied explicitly. Fixed equipment stays fixed unless selected through the study's equipment policy.

The production response is limit-aware, with normal operating bounds and full load unless the caller explicitly chooses another supported contract. Relief, emergency limits and alternate response policies must not be inferred from a solver setting or from this tutorial. Preserve the complete study contract.

Optional headroom objective

The usual base loss objective is not a fuel-cost curve. An optional reserve-aware policy first maximizes a common normalized upward-headroom variable $h$ before optional loss polishing. For generator $g$, let $P_g$ be base active output and $[L_g,U_g]$ its participating effective active interval, all in pu. The constraint is $P_g+(U_g-L_g)h\le U_g$. Variable $h$ is dimensionless.

objective_policy=:reserve stops after the reserve stage; :reserve_loss adds loss polishing; the usual default remains :loss. Headroom is a dispatch preference, not a guarantee of delivering reserve through every outage or a substitute for security constraints.

Independent checks and model limits

Require native success and validate_multi_period_security(studies, result).valid. Replay checks every period/state, exact controls, capability, equipment, response, ramps, target sharing and the declared objective. Store the input studies as well as the result; a result's policy fields are not an independent specification.

AVR/droop labels do not instantiate synchronous-machine, governor, PLL, grid-forming or grid-following dynamics. Steady-state security does not establish frequency response, transient stability, discrete movement or a dynamic transition.
